03Membership

Plans are set on
the phone, not here.

Monthly, quarterly, annual, a student rate, maybe a day pass. Nothing in a public listing tells you what any of it costs today, so nothing here pretends to.

A stale price
is worse than none.

Study libraries price by the seat, the month and the season. A rate quoted in a listing two years ago can be wrong in both directions, and it costs you a wasted trip either way.

So this page does not print a price, a deposit, or a refund rule. It prints the number and the three things worth asking, which is what a front desk would rather tell you anyway.

If you are comparing two libraries, the useful figures are the ones you write down during the call: the plan length, what it covers, and whether a seat is actually free tonight.

02How a call goes

Three sentences,
then you know.

  1. 01

    Ask what is open now

    Plan names and lengths change. Start with what is available this month, not last year.

  2. 02

    Ask what it covers

    Which hours, which seat, whether the material is yours or theirs. Get the inclusions in one sentence.

  3. 03

    Ask about tonight

    A free seat on Tuesday evening says nothing about Tuesday at 2 a.m. Ask about the hour you mean.

03What not to expect

A page
without prices.

There is no booking form and no online payment here. A seat held by a form that the library never sees would be a worse promise than a phone call.

No WhatsApp button either: the number is published, the messaging route is not confirmed, and a dead link is not a service.

What there is: the address, the hours, the map, and a phone number that public listings place inside a 24-hour library.
